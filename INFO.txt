Project Overview
Project Green is a location-based land information platform designed to make land research simpler, more accessible, and transparent.

The central idea is to allow a user to visit a land parcel, capture a geotagged photograph using a smartphone, and retrieve relevant land-related information using its geographical coordinates (latitude and longitude).

The system aims to connect geographical location data with available government land-record sources and other authorized data services to help users understand more about a property before making land-related decisions.

Instead of manually searching through multiple sources and navigating complicated record systems, users will have a centralized platform to begin their land research.

Our core concept:

A photograph → GPS coordinates → Land parcel identification → Data retrieval → Property information.

2. Why Are We Building Project Green?
Land-related research can be complicated, especially for people who are unfamiliar with government portals and property-record procedures.

Some of the challenges include:
- Difficulty identifying the exact land parcel from its physical location.
- Limited awareness of available government land-record services.
- Having to visit multiple portals to gather property information.
- Difficulty understanding survey numbers and geographical boundaries.
- Challenges in connecting a physical plot of land with its corresponding digital records.
- Risk of making property decisions without sufficient preliminary information.
Project Green aims to address these challenges by providing a simpler, location-based starting point for land research.
3. Our Main Aim
Our primary aim is to develop a smart platform that connects real-world land locations with digitally accessible property information.

We want to:
1. Simplify the process of identifying land parcels.
2. Use geotagged photographs to obtain geographical coordinates.
3. Connect GPS coordinates with available cadastral maps and land records.
4. Integrate accessible Karnataka government land-information sources.
5. Present retrieved information through a simple, understandable interface.
6. Help people conduct preliminary property research before making decisions.

4. How Will the System Work?
The proposed system will follow a sequence of operations.
Step 1: Capturing the Land Photograph
- The user visits the land parcel.
- Opens Project Green using a smartphone.
- Captures a photograph through the application.
- The application requests location permission and obtains GPS coordinates.
- The image and its associated location information are sent to the backend.
Step 2: Extracting Geographical Coordinates
The system obtains:
- Latitude
- Longitude
- Timestamp
- Image metadata, where available
For example:
Parameter	Sample value
Latitude	12.2958
Longitude	76.6394
Timestamp	Capture time
Image	Uploaded land photograph

These coordinates help establish the approximate geographical location of the photographed land.

Important: GPS coordinates identify a location, not the legal identity or ownership of a land parcel. The system must match the coordinates against cadastral maps or other authorized parcel data to identify a survey number.
Step 3: Land Parcel Identification
This is one of the most important parts of our project.

The geographical coordinates will be processed using GIS (Geographic Information System) techniques.

Our system will attempt to:
- Locate the user's position on a digital map.
- Compare the coordinates with available land parcel boundaries.
- Identify the corresponding parcel, if reliable boundary data is available.
- Retrieve the associated survey number or parcel identifier.
If accurate parcel boundaries are unavailable, the system should display the approximate location rather than claim an exact parcel match.
Step 4: Land Information Retrieval
Once a parcel is identified, the system will attempt to retrieve relevant information through available data sources.

Our initial research will focus on Karnataka.

Potential information sources include:
- Bhoomi: RTC and related land-record information.
- Mojini: Survey and land measurement-related information.
- e-Khata: Relevant urban property records, where accessible.
- Government cadastral maps and other authorized GIS datasets.
We will investigate available APIs, official portals, data-access policies and integration possibilities.

Not all these sources necessarily provide public APIs. Where direct integration is unavailable, we will explore authorized alternatives rather than assume unrestricted access.
Step 5: Displaying the Information
The retrieved information will be organized into a user-friendly dashboard.

The proposed dashboard may display:
Information	Purpose
Location	Geographical position of land
Survey number	Parcel identification
Village / Taluk / District	Administrative location
RTC details	Available land-record information
Ownership details	Officially accessible recorded details
Land classification	Agricultural or other recorded classification
Parcel boundaries	Available mapped boundaries
Record status	Source and date of retrieved information

The exact fields will depend on the availability and permissions of the underlying datasets.
5. Main Objectives and Expected Outcomes
Objective	Expected outcome
Geotagged photography	Capture a land image with its location
GPS extraction	Obtain accurate geographical coordinates
GIS integration	Map the location against available land parcels
Government data integration	Retrieve relevant available land records
Information processing	Organize records into understandable sections
User-friendly interface	Make land research easier for ordinary users
Transparency	Show the source and freshness of retrieved information

Our ultimate objective is to develop a working prototype that demonstrates how a physical land location can be connected to digital land information.
